---
title: Templates
description: Save a Compose app once and create stacks from it again and again.
---

A **template** is a complete Compose app: `compose.yaml`, an optional
`.env` and every file next to them. You edit its **draft** and publish
**versions**. Stacks are created from a published version.

## Create a template

1. Open **Templates** and press **New Template**. Enter a name, a short
   description and tags (press Space, Enter or a comma after each tag),
   and press **Create Template**.
2. On the **Files** tab, edit the starter `compose.yaml`, add a `.env` and
   the files your services need.
3. Press **Publish Version**, enter a **Version** such as `1.0.0` and **What
   Changed**. The version's files are frozen; you can keep editing the
   draft.

A template needs a `compose.yaml` (or `docker-compose.yml`) at the top,
must not set a top-level `name:`, and holds up to 32 MB and 5,000 files.

To turn a running app into a template, open the stack and choose **More
Stack Actions → Save as Template**. Save it as a new template, or into an
existing template's draft (this replaces the draft). Leave out data folders
such as databases or uploads.

## Create a stack from a template

1. Open **Stacks → Create Stack ▾ → Create Stack From Template**, or press
   **Create Stack** on a template's page.
2. Pick the template, its **Version**, the **Environment** and a **Name**.
3. Change the `.env` values for this stack, such as passwords.
4. Press **Create Stack**, or tick **Deploy After Creating** and press
   **Create and Deploy**.

The stack gets its own copy of the files. Changing the template later does
not change the stack.

## Versions

The **Versions** tab lists every published version.

- **Restore Draft** puts a version's files back into the draft.
- **Duplicate as a New Template** makes your own copy of a version.
- Deleting a version does not affect stacks created from it.

Under **Settings** you can upload an icon (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or SVG, up
to 256 KB and 1024 × 1024 pixels), and change the details, tags and links.

## Share templates

New templates are **private**. **Settings → Make Public** shares a template
with other Docker Manager installations.

<Callout type="warn" title="Public means public">
  Anyone who knows your Docker Manager's address can download every file of
  every published version of a public template, including `.env`. Remove
  passwords, keys and tokens and publish a clean version first.
</Callout>

To use templates from another Docker Manager:

1. On the other one, copy the address under **Templates → Template Sources
   → Share Your Templates**.
2. On yours, open **Templates → Template Sources**, press **Add Template
   Source** and paste it. The address must use HTTPS. Only the owner can
   add sources.

Its public templates now appear on your **Templates** page, marked with its
name. The list refreshes every 30 minutes, or press the sync button next to
the source.
