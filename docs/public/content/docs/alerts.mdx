---
title: Alerts
description: Problems Docker Manager spots by itself, finished backups, prunes and updates, and how you see and receive them.
---

Docker Manager watches your servers and raises an **alert** when something
needs you: a failing disk, a degraded RAID array, a server that runs hot,
is almost out of disk space or memory or went offline, a scheduled job
that failed or new image updates. It also records a **notification**
whenever a backup or restore, a prune or an update finishes. Both show in
the app and go to your [notification channels](/notifications).

## What raises an alert

| Alert | When | Ends when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Disk health** | A disk is failing (**Critical**), needs attention because of damaged sectors, wear or low spare (**Warning**), or can't be read (**Warning**). A sleeping disk never raises one. | The disk is healthy again. |
| **RAID** | An array has failed or is inactive (**Critical**), or is degraded or rebuilding (**Warning**). A degraded ZFS pool is a **Warning**; a faulted, unavailable, suspended or removed one is **Critical**. | The array or pool is healthy again. |
| **Temperature** | The hottest sensor of a server stays at or above the warning level (**Warning**) or the critical level (**Critical**) for 5 minutes. | It is 3 °C below the warning level. |
| **Disk space** | A server's Docker data, stacks or bind mount disk stays at or above the warning or critical level for 5 minutes. | It is 3 points below the warning level. |
| **Memory** | A server's memory use stays at or above the warning or critical level for 5 minutes. | It is 3 points below the warning level. |
| **Environment offline** | A server has been offline for 5 minutes (**Critical**). | The server is back online. |
| **Failed job** | A scheduled job, or one started with an API token, failed (**Critical**), partly failed or was interrupted (**Warning**). Jobs you start yourself show only in your bell. | The next run of the same job succeeds. |
| **Updates available** | An update check finds newer images (**Info**). | No update is left. |

A disk or array that is no longer reported for a day, a failed job that
doesn't run again for 7 days, and the alerts of an archived environment
end without a message.

## Change the thresholds

1. Open **Settings → Notifications**.
2. Under **Alert thresholds**, set the **Warning** and **Critical** levels
   of **Temperature (°C)**, **Disk space (% used)** and **Memory (% used)**.
   `0` turns a level off.
3. Press **Save thresholds**.

To use other levels on one server, press **Add override** under
**Environment overrides**, choose the **Environment** and fill in only the
levels you want to change; empty ones use the defaults. Only the owner
can change thresholds.

The defaults are 80 and 90 °C, 85 and 95 % disk space and 90 and 95 %
memory.

## See alerts and notifications

- **Notifications** in the side menu has two tabs. **Notifications** lists
  every finished backup, restore, prune and update by day, with how it
  went (**Done**, **Warning** or **Failed**) and its numbers: what a prune
  reclaimed in total and per containers, images, volumes, networks and
  build cache, what a backup saved, which services an update recreated.
  **Alerts** lists the alerts: choose **Active**, **Dismissed** or
  **Resolved**, a kind or an environment.
- The **bell** at the top counts the alerts and job messages you haven't
  dismissed. The count stays until you dismiss them.
- Click an alert or notification to open what it is about: the server's
  **System** tab, the server, the job or the update policy.
- The dashboard's **Needs attention** shows how many disks and RAID arrays
  need attention, and an environment's page shows a notice with **View
  disks**.

You see an alert when you may see what it is about: the server's system
information for disks and RAID, its host stats for temperature, disk
space and memory, the server for offline alerts, the job, or the update
policy. You see a notification when you may see its job.

## Dismiss an alert

1. Press **Dismiss** next to the alert on the **Alerts** tab, or **×**
   next to it in the bell. **Dismiss all** (on the tab or in the bell)
   dismisses every alert you see.
2. The alert leaves the bell, the dashboard and the environment's notice
   for everyone. It stays under **Dismissed** until the problem is gone.

If the problem gets worse (a higher severity, or a new problem such as
another failed disk in the array), the alert opens again and is sent
again.

## Get them as messages

Add a [notification channel](/notifications) and tick what it should send
under **What to send**. A channel gets:

- a message when an alert starts, and again when it gets worse, for the
  levels you ticked (**Warning**, **Critical**),
- a **Resolved:** message when the problem is gone, if you ticked
  **Resolved** and the channel got the alert,
- a message when a backup, prune or update finishes, for the outcomes you
  ticked.

Messages start with your Docker Manager's name, say what went wrong and
what to do, list the numbers, and link to the page they are about.
Several at once arrive as one message. If a channel can't be reached,
Docker Manager tries again for a day.

## Good to know

- A failed scheduled backup, prune or update shows as an alert, but its
  message comes from the **Backups and restores**, **Prune** or **Image
  updates** row of **What to send**, not from **Other failed jobs**.
- Dismissing for everyone needs the **Dismiss alerts** permission (the
  **Operator** and **Admin** presets have it). Without it, dismissing in
  the bell hides the alert only in your browser.
- Change how long a server may be offline with
  `DOCKER_MANAGER_ALERT_OFFLINE_GRACE` (see
  [Configuration](/configuration#history-and-monitoring)). After a restart
  of Docker Manager, servers get that full time to reconnect.
- In rare cases a message arrives twice.
- Resolved alerts and notifications stay for 90 days.
