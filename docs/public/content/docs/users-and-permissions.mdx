---
title: Users and permissions
description: Invite people and decide what each of them may see and do.
---

The person who set up Docker Manager is its **owner**. The owner can do
everything and is the only one who manages people and permissions, under
**Access** in the menu. Nobody can sign up on their own.

## Invite someone

1. Open **Access** and press **Invite user**.
2. Under **Options** you can enter an **Email** (only that address can
   then use the link; Docker Manager sends no email) and **Expires after
   (hours)** (72 hours by default).
3. Press **Create invite link**, copy the link and send it yourself.

The link works once and is shown only once. The person opens it, chooses a
username and password, and presses **Create account**.

New people see nothing until you give them access. The **Invitations** tab
lists your links; **Revoke** stops a link that was not used yet.

## Groups

Every person belongs to exactly one **group**, and the group's rules decide
what its members may do. New accounts join the group marked **Default for
new users**. At first, that is **Restricted**, which gives no access.

- **Create group** makes a new group, without access.
- **Add members** on a group's page moves people into it.
- A group's menu can **Rename group**, **Make default for new users** or
  **Delete group** (only an empty group that is not the default).

## Give permissions

On a group's page, pick where the rules apply in the tree on the left:

- **All resources**: everything, including servers you add later,
- one environment, with everything on it,
- or a single stack, container, volume, policy and so on.

Then choose **Allow** or **Deny** for each action. Without a rule, an
action is denied. The most specific rule decides: a rule on one stack wins
over a rule on its environment, which wins over **All resources**. Risky
actions are marked **High risk**.

To start quickly, pick a preset under **Start from**:

- **Viewer**: sees everything, changes nothing. Logs, files and secrets
  stay hidden.
- **Operator**: also reads logs, and can start, stop, restart and deploy.
  It can't delete anything.
- **Admin**: can do everything at that place, risky actions included.

Then adjust single actions if you need to. Nothing changes until you press
**Save permissions** and confirm the list of changes. The members' open
pages then reload with their new permissions.

## One person's page

Open a person under **Access** to:

- **Move user** to another group,
- set **Overrides**: rules for just this person. They win over every rule
  of the group,
- see their **Effective access**, and **View as this user**,
- see their **Sessions**: the devices they are signed in on. **Sign out**
  signs out one device.

The **Account actions** menu can:

- **Disable account** (and **Enable account**). This signs them out and
  revokes their API tokens for good.
- **Sign out everywhere**.
- **Reset sign-in factors**: removes their authenticator app, passkeys and
  recovery codes, so they can set them up again.
- **Create password reset link**: a link that works once, for 24 hours by
  default. Send it to them yourself.
- **Delete account**. They can only come back with a new invitation.

## What only the owner can do

Some things can't be given to anyone else: managing people, groups and the
sign-in policy, other people's API tokens, registry connections and Git
credentials, template sources, update and maintenance policies for all
environments, backups of the manager state, importing backups, restoring
Docker Manager and downloading the support bundle.

The owner account can't be disabled or deleted, and rules never apply to
it. If you lose access to it, see
[Sign-in and security](/docs/sign-in-and-security#locked-out-of-the-owner-account).
